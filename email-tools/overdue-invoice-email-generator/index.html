A reliable structure

What makes this message work

Include verifiable details

Invoice number, amount, due date, and payment link reduce unnecessary replies.

Assume a solvable problem first

The first reminder can account for oversight or processing delay without sounding weak.

Escalate predictably

Later reminders should state the next step and date based on your agreement.

Why it matters

Clear enough to act on. Human enough to trust.

Emotion makes collection harder. Facts, documentation, and a consistent escalation process protect both the relationship and your cash flow.

Do not threaten fees or legal action that are not supported by the contract and local law.

First reminder

Short and neutral

Invoice 1048 for $2,400 was due Friday. Could you confirm whether payment is in progress?

Second reminder

Request a date

The invoice is now seven days overdue. Please confirm the payment date by tomorrow.

Escalation

State the next step

If payment is not received by Monday, we will pause new work under the payment terms.

Do

Keep these

  • Include invoice details
  • Attach or link the invoice
  • Use a consistent escalation path
Avoid

Remove these

  • Make it personal
  • Use unsupported threats
  • Hide the amount or due date
Questions

overdue invoice email generator FAQ

When should I send the first overdue reminder?

Often the first business day after the due date, unless your contract or relationship suggests otherwise.

Should I add a late fee?

Only when the fee is part of the agreed terms and permitted by applicable law.

What if they dispute the invoice?

Pause collection language, identify the disputed line item, and establish a date to resolve it.
