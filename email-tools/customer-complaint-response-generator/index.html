A reliable structure

What makes this message work

Repeat the real issue

Use the customer’s specific problem so the response does not feel automated.

Take appropriate ownership

Do not argue about intent when the outcome is clear.

Offer a bounded resolution

Give the options, owner, and expected timing.

Why it matters

Clear enough to act on. Human enough to trust.

Empathy without a solution sounds scripted. A solution without acknowledgement sounds dismissive. Effective service recovery needs both.

Avoid promising compensation or outcomes outside your authority. Escalate when the requested remedy requires approval.

Damaged order

Replacement or refund

I am sorry the item arrived damaged. I can ship a replacement today or issue a full refund—whichever you prefer.

Delay

Late service

We missed the promised delivery window. I have upgraded shipping and will send the tracking number this afternoon.

Billing

Incorrect charge

You were charged twice. I have reversed the duplicate payment; it should appear within five business days.

Do

Keep these

  • Name the problem
  • Give real options
  • Include timing
Avoid

Remove these

  • Blame the customer
  • Use canned empathy alone
  • Promise what you cannot approve
Questions

customer complaint response generator FAQ

Should I apologize if the company is not at fault?

You can acknowledge the frustrating experience without admitting facts that are not established.

How fast should complaints be answered?

Respond quickly enough to show ownership. If investigation is required, acknowledge the complaint and give an update time.

Should I offer a refund immediately?

Follow your policy and authority. Offer the most useful remedy you can actually deliver.
