Clarity before commitment

Ask the question that prevents the wrong answer.

When a request is incomplete, guessing creates expensive mistakes. A useful clarification email explains what you already understand, lists the missing details, and shows what happens after they reply.

A reliable structure

What makes this message work

Show what you understand

Restate the request briefly so the recipient can correct any wrong assumption.

Group the missing details

Use two or three specific questions instead of one large vague question.

Explain the dependency

Say whether the answer affects price, timing, scope, or your ability to proceed.

Why it matters

Clear enough to act on. Human enough to trust.

Clarification is not resistance. It is a way to protect the deadline and avoid producing work based on a hidden assumption.

Questions are easier to answer when they use the recipient’s language and request only information that changes the outcome.

Project

Clarify scope

Should the report cover the US launch only, or all three markets? And is the deadline for a draft or final version?

Quote

Price accurately

How many users need access, and do you need migration included? Those two details determine the quote.

Application

Missing document

I received the form but not the signed authorization. Could you send that page so I can complete the review?

Do

Keep these

  • Restate your understanding
  • Ask numbered questions
  • Explain why the answer matters
Avoid

Remove these

  • Ask for everything at once
  • Use accusatory language
  • Proceed on a risky assumption
Questions

ask for more information email FAQ

How do I ask for information politely?

Explain what you are trying to complete, ask specific questions, and make the next step clear.

Should I use bullet points?

Yes, especially when requesting three or more independent details.

What if the request is urgent?

State the exact deadline and identify the minimum information required to keep it.
